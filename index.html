RegBot: retrieval-augmented generation over UK financial services regulation

Individual Generative AI & AI Applications PythonChromaDB BM25Gradio
Problem
A compliance answer has to be traceable to a named provision, not paraphrased from a model's memory. Regulation is also amended over time, so a model with fixed weights becomes outdated without any signal.
Approach
Indexed the Payment Services Regulations 2017, Electronic Money Regulations 2011 and Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. Built two pipelines sharing the same model and prompt so only retrieval differed: a baseline, and an enhanced version using HyDE query expansion, hybrid vector and BM25 retrieval at 60/40, and cross-encoder reranking.
Result
Answer correctness rose 22%. Segmenting by question type showed the gain sat almost entirely on ambiguous questions rather than straightforward ones, which is consistent with what these three techniques were selected to address.

What didn't work

On an insolvency question the HyDE passage cited the Insolvency Act 1986, which is not in the corpus. That pulled retrieval away from the relevant safeguarding provisions and the baseline performed better. Separately, the reported recall of 0.016 is an artefact of retrieving 3 chunks from a corpus of 527, not a real weakness. Measuring it properly would need a hand-labelled relevance set.

Loan review policy evaluation for LendingClub: A/B testing, segmentation and deep learning

Team of 6 Advanced Data Analysis A/B testingK-means PyTorchPython
Problem
A consumer lender has to set an approval threshold somewhere. Loosen it and you write more business but absorb more defaults. Tighten it and you protect the book but turn away loans that would have repaid. The question is where the line should sit, and whether one line works for everybody.
Approach
Three methods stacked on 49,969 LendingClub loans, using only variables known at origination. A simulated A/B test of two approval rules with stated hypotheses and a two-proportion z-test. K-means segmentation on borrower characteristics, with k chosen by elbow and silhouette. A neural network scoring individual default probability, with three architectures of increasing depth compared against each other.
Result
The stricter policy cut the default rate from 13.51% to 11.33%, highly significant at z = 8.0. But Cohen's h of 0.066 says the effect is small, and the cost is 12,533 loans not written. Segmentation then showed the benefit is not evenly spread: the financially stretched cluster gained most, the conservative cluster barely at all. One threshold for everyone is the wrong shape of answer.

The finding I would lead with

All three neural networks landed between AUC 0.666 and 0.669. Adding depth and regularisation changed nothing. SMOTE and undersampling both made results worse than simple class weighting. The honest conclusion was that better predictions need better data at application, not a bigger model. The weakest segment to predict was also the riskiest one, which is where a lender would most want accuracy.

Photonix Studio: a relational database for supplier selection

Team of 5 · I led the work Data Management SQLSQLitePython
Problem
A consumer electronics brand picked suppliers on unit cost and lead time alone. Quality outcomes were never connected back to specific suppliers, so nobody could see what the cheap option actually cost.
Approach
Designed a 13-table schema in third normal form linking suppliers, products, orders, reviews and returns, implemented in SQLite, and populated it with 12,607 synthetic records generated in Python. The five analytical queries were written as a deliberate sequence: build the case for cheap suppliers, then test it against the quality evidence.
Result
The two cheapest suppliers produced 65% of all refunds. Once refunds were deducted, the cheapest supplier returned £6,974 net profit on £129,538 of revenue, while the premium supplier returned £21,482 on less revenue. The cost advantage reversed entirely.

Predicting business credit applicants for Universal Plus Bank

Team of 7 · I delivered the client pitch Analytics in Practice PythonXGBoostCRISP-DM
Problem
A bank wanted to identify customers likely to apply for business credit so it could engage them before they went elsewhere. Only about 1 in 10 customers ever applies, so the positive class was small and easy to miss.
Approach
Followed CRISP-DM across 100,000 records and 200 anonymised features. Dropped a variable with 40% missingness rather than impute it, balanced the training set with random undersampling and SMOTE, then compared six classifiers and tuned the decision threshold for F1 rather than accepting the default.
Result
XGBoost led on most metrics. The commercially useful finding was not the F1 score. It was that contacting the top-ranked 30% of customers captures over 80% of future applicants, which turns a modest classifier into a workable targeting policy.

Limitations

Accuracy of 0.905 looks strong but means little at 90:10 imbalance, since a model predicting "no" every time scores 0.90. The evaluation therefore relies on precision-recall curves and cumulative gain rather than accuracy. Minority-class performance remains constrained by the imbalance and by the limited signal in many of the anonymised features.

Halfords Verified Service Passport: a digital service proposal

Individual Digital Business Services Service designPrimary research
Problem
Halfords runs 370 stores and 496 garages with a strong online booking journey. The information a service generates, covering inspection findings, parts, warranties and advisories, stays attached to individual transactions. There is no persistent record of the vehicle itself.
Approach
Mapped the B2C journey to locate exactly where digital support falls away, then evidenced it with two forms of primary observation: a structured walkthrough of the live account interface, and analysis of seven one-star Autocentres reviews from May to July 2026. Benchmarked against Kwik Fit's Vehicle Health Check.
Result
Proposed a vehicle-linked maintenance record inside the existing customer account, with a technology comparison, implementation lifecycle, and mitigations for the top two risks. Graded among the highest in the cohort.
Note on method

This project started from an observed service gap rather than a dataset. The friction point had to be evidenced through primary research before any solution was proposed.

Household expenditure and podcast performance: regression analysis in R

Individual Business Statistics RRegression Interaction effects
Problem
Two datasets, two questions of the same shape. What drives weekly food spending in English households, and what drives daily listens for a podcast. In both cases the interesting question is not whether a variable matters, but whether its effect changes depending on something else.
Approach
Multiple linear regression with interaction terms rather than main effects alone. For households, testing whether the relationship between alcohol spending and food spending shifts with debt repayment levels. For podcasts, testing whether review score, review volume and subscriber count behave differently across genres. Distributions and model assumptions were checked before interpreting any coefficient.
Result
Food expenditure tracked other financial commitments, housing costs, alcohol and debt repayment, far more closely than it tracked household demographics such as size or number of children. The model was statistically significant but left much of the variation unexplained, which is reported plainly rather than dressed up.
