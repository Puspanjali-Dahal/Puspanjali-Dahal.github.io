RegBot — retrieval over UK financial services regulation

Individual projectPython RAGFinTech / RegTech

Compliance questions have a specific problem: the answer has to be traceable to an actual provision, not paraphrased from a model's memory. I built a retrieval system over three pieces of UK legislation — the Payment Services Regulations 2017, the Electronic Money Regulations 2011, and the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 — so a product owner can ask a plain question and get an answer with the regulation number attached.

How it works. A baseline pipeline embeds the question and pulls the three nearest chunks. The enhanced pipeline changes three things: HyDE drafts a hypothetical regulatory passage and searches with that instead of the raw question, hybrid retrieval blends vector similarity with BM25 keyword search at 60/40, and a cross-encoder reranker picks the final three from ten candidates. Both pipelines share the same model and prompt, so every difference comes from retrieval alone.

The result that matters. On one ambiguous question about consent requirements, the baseline retrieved nothing relevant at all — precision 0.00 — and answered from the wrong statute. The enhanced pipeline hit precision 1.00 and cited the exact provision, section 67(2)(b) on the required form of consent. That is the gap these three techniques were chosen to close, and it closed.

What didn't work, and why
  • HyDE can steer retrieval the wrong way. On an insolvency question the hypothetical passage referenced the Insolvency Act 1986, which isn't in the corpus. It pulled search away from the relevant safeguarding provisions and the baseline outperformed. A hypothetical document that's slightly off-target is worse than none.
  • The recall figure of 0.016 is an evaluation artefact, not a finding. Retrieving 3 chunks from 527 makes corpus-wide recall structurally tiny regardless of quality. A real measure needs a hand-labelled relevance set.
  • Grounding fell slightly, 0.375 to 0.359. The enhanced pipeline synthesised across chunks instead of quoting one. For a compliance reader that's arguably better, which makes grounding the less useful metric here.
