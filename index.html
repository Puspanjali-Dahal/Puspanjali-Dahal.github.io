LendingClub

Team project
Credit Risk × Analytics
A test of two loan approval policies, measuring what a lender gains in lower defaults and gives up in lending volume.
A stricter policy cut the default rate by 2.18 percentage points, at the cost of 12,533 loans not written. The benefit was concentrated in one borrower segment, not spread evenly.
Credit RiskExperimentationPython
Problem
Loosen approval criteria and you write more business but absorb more defaults. Tighten them and you protect the book but turn away loans that would have repaid.
Approach
A/B tested two approval policies on 49,969 loans with stated hypotheses, then segmented borrowers to see whether one threshold suits everyone, then scored applicants individually with a neural network.
Trade-off
Lower losses are paid for in forgone lending. The result was statistically decisive but a small effect size, which is the difference between significant and important.

Photonix

Team project, I led the work
Supplier Analysis × Profitability
A database that connects returns and reviews back to the supplier who made the product, to show what a cheap supplier really costs.
The two lowest-cost suppliers generated 65% of all refunds. Once returns were counted, the premium supplier returned 3× the net profit of the cheapest.
Business AnalyticsSQLProfitability
Problem
Suppliers were chosen on unit cost and lead time. Quality outcomes were never linked back to the supplier who made the product, so nobody could see what cheap actually cost.
Approach
Designed a 13-table database in third normal form linking suppliers, orders, reviews and returns, populated it with 12,607 records, then wrote five queries as a deliberate sequence: build the case for cheap suppliers, then test it against the quality evidence.
Decision
Evaluate suppliers on total profitability rather than purchase price.

Universal Plus Bank

Team project
Banking × Predictive Analytics
A model that ranks bank customers by how likely they are to apply for business credit, so outreach goes to the right people first.
Contacting the top-ranked 30% of customers captures over 80% of future credit applicants, turning a modest classifier into a workable outreach policy.
BankingPredictive AnalyticsPython
Problem
Only around 1 customer in 10 ever applies for business credit. The bank needed to know where to spend limited outreach rather than contact everyone.
Approach
Worked through CRISP-DM on 100,000 records and 200 features. Dropped a variable at 40% missingness rather than impute it, balanced the training set, compared six classifiers, and tuned the decision threshold rather than accepting the default. I delivered the client-facing pitch.
Decision
Rank customers by score and work down the list. The bank does not need to know exactly who will apply, only who to call first.
